01

What BravePay is

BravePay is a mobile banking product for Nigerian individuals and small businesses, built around one idea: the bank should also run the business, not just hold its money.

Most banking apps stop at the account. Ours carries invoicing, payroll and expense tracking inside the same app, for the customers least likely to be running proper finance software — traders, small firms, sole operators. That combination is the product, and it is the part competitors cannot copy quickly.

Underneath, five separate systems cooperate to deliver it. They are described below, but the summary is short: a mobile app, a website, a back end that holds the business logic, a service that sends messages, and a console the team uses to run operations.

04

The business tools

This is where BravePay stops being another banking app.

A small business owner in Nigeria typically runs invoicing on paper or a phone note, pays staff by transferring one by one, and has no idea what they spent last month. All three problems sit inside our app, next to the money.

Getting paid

Invoicing

Create and send an invoice in minutes, track what is owed, see what is overdue, mark it paid. Search, sorting and one-swipe actions on a phone.

Paying people

Payroll

Register the company, add staff, run payroll on a schedule. Employees complete their own details through a link, so the owner is not typing bank details by hand.

Knowing where it went

Expense tracking

Categorise spending, attach receipts, and see it as a chart rather than a list. Monthly trends and category breakdowns.

Why it matters

The commercial case

A customer whose invoices and payroll live in our app does not casually switch banks. These tools cost us nothing per transaction and make the account materially harder to leave.

Anyone can offer an account. Very few offer the account and the bookkeeping in the same place.

05

How money will move

Holding customer deposits is a licensed, examined activity. Rather than build that ourselves, we are putting a proven core banking platform underneath BravePay.

The arrangement is simple to state. BravePay is the shopfront. The banking engine is the back office. NIBSS — the national system every Nigerian bank connects to — is the road between banks. A customer sees only BravePay.

What that gets us

Valid account numbers, a proper ledger, transfers to and from every Nigerian bank, and the monthly reports the regulator requires — none of which we have to build or defend. It also brings cards, loans, fixed deposits and standing orders as part of the platform rather than as future projects.

What stays ours is the app, the business tools, the insight and the customer relationship — which is the part worth owning.

Two companion briefings cover this in more depth: How BravePay Moves Money explains it from scratch, and Building BravePay on BankOne sets out what changes, roughly what it takes, and what has to be settled first.

The honest summary

The product is built. The banking underneath it is the remaining work. Everything a customer touches — the app, the tools, the console, the website — exists and runs. What is not yet connected is the licensed engine that will hold real accounts and move money between banks, and that is a defined piece of work rather than an open question.

On security

We run internal review of the code that handles money, and we act on what it finds. A recent review identified a flaw in how simultaneous transactions were handled — the kind that only appears under load — and it was corrected and covered by automated tests within the same day. Those tests were then verified by deliberately reintroducing the fault to confirm they would catch it.

That is the standard we hold: find it ourselves, fix it properly, and prove the fix works. Ahead of launch the integration will also go through independent security testing as a defined stage, not an afterthought.

08

What happens next

The banking integration is the next body of work. It runs in ten stages over roughly nine months of elapsed time, with early stages deliberately front-loading the questions that could change the plan.

StageWhat it delivers
FirstAccess, credentials and a working test connection. The three questions that could change the plan are answered here.
ThenReal account numbers for new customers, and identity checks running against the engine.
ThenTransfers — within BravePay first, then to other banks.
ThenDaily reconciliation and the operations queue that goes with it.
FinallyIndependent security testing, provider sign-off, staff training, then a staged rollout starting with our own team.

How we go live without a bad weekend

Nothing switches at once. First we read from the engine and compare against our own figures for a week without acting. Then new customers only. Then transfers, most recoverable type first. The old path stays in place until each step has been proven, with a rehearsed way back at every stage.

09

What we need decided

Four things, none of which engineering can settle alone.

  1. Provider confirmations. Three questions are with our banking provider covering automatic notification of incoming money, secure connections, and where our data is physically stored. The third matters because from 1 January 2027 Nigerian payment data must be held in Nigeria.
  2. Bill payments supplier. Keep the current one or move to the banking platform's. Worth comparing coverage before choosing — it affects how much daily reconciliation work we carry.
  3. Operations resourcing. Daily reconciliation and a dispute queue need people, not just software. Worth deciding who owns that before launch rather than after.
  4. Launch sequencing. Which customer group goes first, and what evidence we require before widening.

If you read only one paragraph

The product is built and running. We are buying the licensed banking underneath it rather than constructing it, which removes years of work and the burden of defending a home-made ledger to a regulator. What remains ours is the app, the business tools and the customer relationship — the parts that actually differentiate us. The next milestone is the banking integration, and the work is planned stage by stage with the riskiest questions answered first.
