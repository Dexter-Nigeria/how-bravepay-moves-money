The decision

What we are actually choosing

BankOne becomes the engine that holds accounts, balances and the official record of every naira. BravePay becomes the product that customers use.

Put plainly: we stop trying to be a bank's back office and go back to being a very good bank's front end. That is not a retreat. The back office is expensive, heavily regulated, and identical at every institution. The front end is the only part customers ever choose between.

BankOne owns the money. BravePay owns everything that is not money.

This is the sentence to remember. Almost every question that comes up during the project — should this live here or there, who is responsible when something breaks, what do we build next — answers itself against that line.

Why not build it ourselves?

Asked and costed properly in Build or Buy the Core. The short answer: around 300–400 person-months, two to three years, and a team of twelve — and the software is only about 40% of the work. The rest is certification, compliance and operational track record, none of which money shortens.

Why the line falls exactly there

Three reasons, and they reinforce each other. BankOne holds the licence-grade ledger and files the regulator's monthly returns. Settlement between banks happens at institution level, not app level, so it was never going to be ours. And our current internal record of balances would not survive an examination — it was built as a product feature, not as a bank's books.

What we gain

Capability we get without building it

Everything below already exists and works today for hundreds of institutions. On the day we go live, it is available to us.

Core banking

  • Real account numbers
  • Balances and a proper ledger
  • Transfers within and between banks
  • Account statements
  • Regulatory returns

Products we do not have yet

  • Debit cards, issued properly
  • Loans, with repayment schedules
  • Fixed deposits
  • Standing orders and overdrafts
  • Freezing, holds and restrictions

The second column is worth pausing on. Cards, loans and fixed deposits are not stretch goals we are deferring — they arrive as part of the platform. What we build is the customer-facing product around them, which is a matter of weeks rather than the years it would take to build the underlying banking.

A side benefit worth naming

We currently store card details ourselves. Moving cards onto the platform removes that from our systems entirely — which removes a large, expensive compliance burden along with it. This is one of the clearest wins in the whole move.

What is ours

What we still have to build

Five things. This is the honest scope of work.

  1. The connection itself. A single, well-controlled link between our systems and the banking engine, through which everything passes.
  2. A record of every instruction. Written down before anything is sent, so an unclear outcome can always be chased to a definite answer.
  3. Checking for incoming money. Because nothing tells us automatically — see below.
  4. Daily reconciliation. Comparing our records against the engine's, every day, and raising anything that does not match.
  5. Reporting and insight. Spending categories, trends, business intelligence. The engine holds the data; making it useful is ours.

Notice what is not on that list: ledgers, account numbers, settlement, card issuance, regulatory filing. That is the trade we are making, and it is a good one.

Constraints

Three things that shape the work

Most of the effort and most of the risk trace back to these. They are worth understanding even if the detail is not.

1 · Nothing tells us when money arrives

When someone at another bank sends money to a BravePay customer, it lands correctly and is never at risk. But the banking engine does not notify us — so to alert the customer, our systems have to keep checking.

Checking every account constantly would be slow and expensive. The workable approach is to check different customers at different rates: people using the app right now get checked every half-minute or so, recently active customers every few minutes, everyone else overnight. Fast alerts for the people most likely to be waiting, without the cost of checking everyone.

This is the most valuable question we can ask

Before building all of that, we are asking our provider directly whether any automatic notification exists. If yes, a meaningful chunk of work disappears and customers get instant alerts. If no, we build the checking system. Either way it works — but the answer changes the cost, so we want it before we start.

2 · Every connection has to be the secured kind

Connections carrying customer information must be the sealed, scrambled kind rather than the open kind. This protects names, dates of birth, identity numbers and account numbers as they move between systems.

We treat written confirmation of this as a condition of launch, not an assumption. Two obligations make it non-negotiable: the CBN's cybersecurity rules for institutions like ours have applied since January 2023, and from 1 January 2027 Nigerian payment data must be stored and handled inside Nigeria — a rule that names microfinance banks specifically.

3 · Every instruction needs a short, unique tag

Every transfer carries a short reference. It sounds trivial and it is load-bearing: that tag is how we ask "what happened to this one?" without accidentally causing the payment to happen twice.

The tag has a strict length limit, and ours are currently too long. Fixing it is quick, but it has to happen before the first transfer is ever attempted.

Operations

When things go wrong

None of this is exotic. Every bank handles the same situations daily. The difference between a good system and a bad one is whether they were planned for or discovered live.

SituationWhat happens
Transfer is slowWe keep asking for the outcome and keep the customer informed. Money stays held, not returned.
No answer comes backWe ask again using the same reference, so it cannot be performed twice.
Customer taps send twiceThe second attempt is rejected automatically. Money can only leave once.
One employee's account is wrong in a payroll runThirty-nine get paid, one is flagged. The run does not fail.
Money went to the wrong bank accountOnce it has left, it cannot be pulled back automatically. Recovery becomes a conversation between two banks — which is why the name check before sending matters so much.
Our records disagree with the engine'sRaised as an exception for a person to investigate. Never quietly corrected.
The engine is unreachableInstructions queue rather than fail. Customers are told we will complete it shortly.

The rule that prevents most losses

We never give money back to a customer until we know for certain the transfer failed. Returning it while the outcome is still unclear feels helpful and is how the same money ends up sent twice.

Delivery

How it gets delivered

Ten stages, ordered so the things most likely to change the plan are settled before serious work begins. Durations assume a small team working alongside normal product work — they are estimates, not commitments.

  1. Onboarding and accessCommercial sign-off, accounts, credentials, test access.2–3 weeks
  2. Test environmentBuild the connection, exercise every feature once, get the three key questions answered in writing.3–4 weeks
  3. Customers and accountsReal account numbers for new signups. Our old numbering is retired.4–5 weeks
  4. Bank lookupsChecking who owns an account before money is sent.2 weeks
  5. TransfersWithin BravePay first, then to other banks.5–6 weeks
  6. Outcomes and checkingHandling unclear results automatically; detecting incoming money.3–4 weeks
  7. ReconciliationDaily book-checking and the exception queue for operations.4–5 weeks
  8. Security testingIndependent testing of the whole integration.3 weeks
  9. Sign-off and trainingProvider approval, live credentials, runbooks, staff training.3–4 weeks
  10. Going liveStaff first, then a rising share of customers, with the books checked clean at each step.4–6 weeks

How we go live without a bad weekend

Nothing flips at once. First we read from the engine and compare against our own figures for a week without acting on them. Then new customers only, so any problem touches the fewest people. Then transfers, cheapest and most recoverable type first. Only then does the old system stop being used — a deliberate, dated decision with a rehearsed way back.

Next

Answer these first

Three questions to our provider, in writing. Everything else can proceed while we wait — but stage three should not begin until these are answered.

  1. Is there any way to be told automatically when money arrives? Changes how much we build and how quickly customers are alerted.
  2. Is every live connection the secured kind? Confirmed in writing before we handle real customer data.
  3. Where is our data physically stored? From 1 January 2027 it has to be Nigeria.

If you only remember one thing

We are buying the parts of a bank that are identical everywhere and expensive to build, so we can spend our time on the parts customers actually choose between. The work that remains genuinely ours — the app, the business tools, the insight, and knowing our books are right — is exactly the work worth doing.
